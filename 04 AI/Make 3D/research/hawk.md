# Hawk: sizes and sources

Mode: research (light). A red-tailed hawk, the common large hawk of open country. Figures for the model are estimates taken from published ranges; the model is a stylised toy, not a measurement.

| Figure | Published | Used in the model | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length (male) | 17.7 to 22.1 in | about 22.5 in, beak tip to tail tip | [All About Birds, Cornell Lab](https://allaboutbirds.org/guide/Red-tailed_Hawk/id) |
| Length (female) | 19.7 to 25.6 in | (inside the range) | same |
| Wingspan | 44.9 to 52.4 in (both sexes) | about 50 in, tip to tip with the wings level | same |
| Weight | 24.3 to 51.5 oz | not modelled | same |

Where sources differ: Wikipedia gives 18 to 26 in long and a 110 to 145 cm (43 to 57 in) span; Audubon gives 17 to 26 in long and 45 to 52 in span. The Cornell figures are the more specific, so they are used. See also [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/birds/red-tailed-hawk) and [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-tailed_hawk).

Estimated, not sourced (proportions chosen by eye for a readable toy): body 6.4 in wide, head 3.4 in wide, wing chord 10 in at the elbow, tail 8 in long, 3.5 in wide at the tip, leg length 4.6 in. Colours are softened pastels (tan back, cream breast, a rusty tail), not field-guide colours.

V07 flight (physics, scaled world): the arena is about a tenth of real size, so g = 150 in/s^2 instead of 386, soaring speed 130 in/s, stoop about 240 in/s, 62 wing beats a lap at 2.4 per second (a real red-tail beats about 3 per second when climbing). These are the author's choices for readable motion, not measurements. V06 flight (stylised, not real time): the loop is about 23 s. Speed follows height, v² = v0² + 2g(zTop − z) with v0 = 15 in/s and g = 10 in/s², giving about 43 in/s at the bottom of the stoop. A real stoop is far faster; the slow loop is chosen so the wings can be read.
