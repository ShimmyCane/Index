# Specs 401 — The specifier's practice

**Level:** CCS
**Written in our own words.** **(verify)** = check against a current publication or code edition.

## 1. What a CCS can do

- Pick the right **method** (descriptive, performance, reference standard, proprietary) for each requirement and say why.
- **Research and evaluate products**: compare data sheets, test reports, listings, warranties, track record, availability, cost.
- **Read reference standards** fully: know the options a standard leaves to the specifier (type, grade, class) and choose them.
- **Turn the code analysis into requirements**: fire ratings, listed assemblies, flame spread, energy, accessibility.
- **Coordinate** the whole book with the drawings and the consultants' sections.
- **Manage change** in CA: substitutions, submittals, RFIs, ASIs.
- **Manage risk**: allocate responsibility clearly; avoid over- and under-specifying.
- **Maintain masters**: build lessons into the office master.

## 2. Code analysis → spec requirements

| Code fact | Spec consequences (examples) |
|---|---|
| **Construction type** (IBC Ch. 6) | Types I–II: noncombustible; wood limited to allowed uses (e.g. fire-retardant-treated where permitted, blocking, nailers) **(verify IBC 603 for edition)**. Types III–V: Division 06 framing sections likely. |
| **Fire-resistance ratings** | Tested assemblies (UL or similar) named in sections or on drawings; `07 81 00` fireproofing; `07 84 00` firestopping; rated doors `08 11 13` + `08 71 00` labels; rated glazing `08 80 00`. |
| **Sprinklered or not** | Affects allowances (opening protection, finishes); `21 13 13` in the book. |
| **Occupancy** | Finish flame-spread classes (`09`), hardware (egress, panic), accessibility (`10`, `08 71 00`). |
| **Energy code / climate zone** | Insulation R-values `07 21 00`; air barrier `07 27 00` and testing; fenestration U-factor/SHGC `08`. |
| **Exterior wall rules** (e.g. combustible components in Types I–IV) | NFPA 285-tested assemblies; WRB and insulation choices limited **(verify edition)**. |
| **Existing building code** (IEBC) | Level of alteration sets what must be upgraded; repair vs. alteration. |

## 3. Product selection and substitutions

- **Basis of design:** name one product as the design basis, list others as acceptable, and set the criteria a substitute must meet.
- **Substitution procedure** (`01 25 00`): when (during bidding vs. after award), what the contractor must submit (comparison, cost, schedule, warranty, effect on other work), what the architect will do, and the time limits.
- **Equal is not same:** a substitution must meet the *performance and compatibility* needs, not only the data sheet.

## 4. Delegated design

- When the contractor's engineer designs part of the work (anchors, curtain wall, stairs, sealant joints, shoring), say so in Part 1 (**Delegated-Design Services**) and Part 2 (**Performance Requirements**), require signed and sealed submittals, and coordinate with the structural engineer.

## 5. Allowances, unit prices, alternates

- **Allowances** for scope the design can't fix yet (e.g. "Allowance for 200 sq. ft. of concrete repair").
- **Unit prices** for unknown quantities (repair work lives on these: repointing per sq. ft., brick replacement per unit, crack repair per lin. ft.). Define the unit, the measurement method and what is included.
- **Alternates** for priced choices (base vs. deduct/add). Describe both conditions completely.
- Each needs an entry in Division 01 *and* a cross-reference in the affected section's Part 1.

## 6. Quality

- **QA** (before/around the work): qualifications, mockups, preconstruction testing, preinstallation meetings.
- **QC** (checking the work): field tests, inspections, special inspections (IBC Ch. 17), manufacturer's field service.
- Say **who pays** for testing and **who hires** the agency, in Division 01.

## 7. Specifying for repair and restoration

Repair work turns a lot of the normal rules around:

- **Existing conditions are the design.** Rely on surveys, probes, condition assessments, testing (mortar analysis, petrography, core samples, hazardous materials). Reference reports as information, not as part of the contract, unless intended **(verify with counsel)**.
- **Quantities are unknown** → unit prices and allowances, with a clear way to measure and verify.
- **Mockups and test panels** set the standard (cleaning methods, repointing, patch color/texture). Require approval before full work starts; keep them as the reference.
- **Compatibility first:** new material must not harm old (mortar softer and more permeable than the masonry; sealants compatible with substrates and coatings; cleaning agents tested).
- **Historic work:** Secretary of the Interior's Standards (preservation, rehabilitation, restoration, reconstruction) and NPS Preservation Briefs. `01 35 91 Historic Treatment Procedures`.
- **Occupied buildings:** phasing, noise and dust, protection, access, hours: `01 10 00`, `01 35 16`, `01 50 00`.
- **Hazardous materials:** lead, asbestos, PCBs in old sealants: Division 02 sections and owner surveys.
- **Sections to know:** `02 41 19 Selective Demolition`, `xx 01 00` maintenance sections (`03 01 30`, `04 01 20`, `05 01 10`, `07 01 50`, `08 01 52`… **(verify each number)**).

## 8. Risk and language

- Avoid **"as directed"**, **"to the satisfaction of the Architect"**: they make the architect the judge without criteria. State the criteria.
- Avoid **guarantees you can't measure**: "watertight" with no test method.
- **Warranties:** special warranties (manufacturer and installer) add to the General Conditions warranty; state the term, start, what is covered, and what isn't.

## 9. Practice

- For one section, list every option a cited reference standard leaves open and choose each with a reason.
- Write a unit-price schedule for a masonry repair scope.
- Take one code-analysis sheet and list every spec consequence.
