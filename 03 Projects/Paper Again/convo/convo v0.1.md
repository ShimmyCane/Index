# Paper Again: convo v0.1

Date: 2026-11-03
Participants: Jimmy, Claude

Context: Growth's 3-D version looked like a mesh with small pieces falling out and tears like mesh holes. Jimmy wants a crumpled, ball-like sheet that turns and flips between increments, with more dramatic and more logical damage, and paper that looks like paper. Renamed to Paper Again, ten increments.

## Answers (round 1)

1. **Motion:** both. The 3-D viewer tumbles the paper between increments; the Increment Viewer frames are a fixed pose.
2. **Look:** realistic paper, not the pastel cartoon. (Jimmy's pastel preference is for the kids' games; this project is a realistic study.)
3. **Method:** physics-style simulation, deterministic (same seed, same result). Damage follows from the forces, not from hand-written dents.
4. **Story:** free choice. Claude picks the events and notes them here.
5. **Decay:** creases and crumpling, tears (fibre-style edges), damp and mould, dirt and burn edges. All four in version 1.

## Claude's choices (for Jimmy to change)

- **Story:** arrival (floating, a boot, a truck, a big crease), wind rolls it into a ball, tear off the top, junk caught in it, damp then stain, two cuts, burn, holes and mould, nest with animals. Every increment ends with a tumble.
- **Environment:** outdoors, as a placeholder.
- **Ball:** the model holds the ball with a soft shell until the first cut. Real paper holds itself by jamming; the model does not reproduce that. Listed as a known limit.
- **Tears and cuts:** on the grid (about 0.35 in), not single fibres.
- **Research:** see `04 AI/Paper Again/research/paper_behaviour.md`. Sources are listed there.
