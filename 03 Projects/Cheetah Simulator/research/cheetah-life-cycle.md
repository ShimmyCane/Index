# Cheetah life cycle: research notes

Written 2026-11-25 for the Cheetah Simulator schematic. Two kinds of fact are kept apart:

- **Sourced** (from the pages linked below, read through a search summary, not the full papers).
- **From general knowledge, to check** (marked ⚑). Verify against a primary source before putting a number on screen.

Numbers vary by place and study. The game should show ranges ("about 3 to 4 months"), never one exact figure, and say so in the "How it really is" panel.

## 1. The life cycle in one line

Born in a hidden den → follow mother and learn to hunt → mother leaves (about 18 months) → siblings stay together for a few months → **females leave the group first (about 2 years) and live alone; males stay with brothers for life (a coalition)** → adults mate → gestation about 3 months → mother raises a litter alone, hiding and moving the cubs → cubs grow up and the cycle repeats.

This matches Jimmy's idea: females go solo and males form groups. One correction to the brief: it is the **females who leave the sibling group; males usually stay with their brothers**. Neither sex "stays with Mum" after about 18 months.

| Den cub | 0 to 6-8 weeks | Born in a secluded spot (long grass, thicket, rock). Mother moves them from hiding place to hiding place to avoid predators. Eyes open in about 4-11 days ⚑. | Learner | 4 to 12 months | Mother brings live young prey (hares, fawns) and lets cubs practise ⚑. Cubs learn what to chase and how | [Educating Cheetah, NWF](https://www.nwf.org/Magazines/National-Wildlife/2003/Educating-Cheetah) |
| Adolescent | 12 to about 18 months | Cubs join hunts. Mother leaves, sometimes suddenly, to start a new breeding cycle | [Animal Diversity](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Acinonyx_jubatus) |
| Sibling band | about 18 to 24 months | Littermates stay together about six more months, then break up | [Panthera](https://panthera.org/blog-post/cheetah-game-thrones) |
| Young female | about 2 years | Leaves her siblings, lives alone with a large home range that overlaps several males' territories | Panthera, [Spatial behaviour studies](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/06/180628120049.htm) |
| Young male | about 2 to 3 years | Stays with brothers (coalition of 2 to 3, often for life). Starts as a **floater**: roams a very large range without holding ground. | Mating | adults | Female's heat is irregular and tied to scent and presence of males. Males that find her stay up to three days. Several males can mate with her over time. Male "stutter bark" is linked to ovulation | [Sciencing](https://sciencing.com/cheetahs-reproduce-4566385.html), [Panthera](https://panthera.org/blog-post/cheetah-game-thrones) |

## 3. How hunting works (for the hunt mini-game)

Sourced:
- Chases are short: one study gives an average of about **38 seconds** per chase, with only about 45 seconds of sprinting a day ([National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/cheetahs-prosper-new-study-debunks-more-old-myths)). So the game's "sprint" should be a brief burst, then rest.
- Success is higher in **open savanna** than in thick bush ([Kruger study](https://bioone.org/journals/wildlife-biology/volume-10/issue-1/wlb.2004.024/Cheetah-Acinonyx-jubatus-feeding-ecology-in-the-Kruger-National-Park/10.2981/wlb.2004.024.full)). Habitat should change how hard a hunt is.
- **Kleptoparasitism**: lions and hyenas steal kills. One study estimates about 1 kill in 10 lost, and cheetahs can absorb about 25% theft by travelling more ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/article/1610196/having-lunch-stolen-all-days-work-cheetahs-scientists-show), [NatGeo](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/phenomena/2014/10/02/cheetahs-prosper-new-study-debunks-more-old-myths/)). So: eat fast, then watch.
- Mothers with cubs hunt more carefully and stay more alert, because large predators are the main cause of cub deaths ([Hilborn et al. 2018](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s00265-018-2481-y)).

From general knowledge, to check ⚑:
- Phases: **spot** (stand on a mound, scan, read the herd: pick the young, old, ill or separated animal) → **stalk** (low, use cover, stop when prey looks up) → **burst** (up to about 100 km/h for a few seconds; a cheetah cannot keep it up) → **trip** (a paw hooks the hind leg) → **hold** (a bite to the throat, held for minutes) → **recover** (it is out of breath, about 20 to 30 minutes) → **eat or be robbed**.
- Daytime hunter (dawn and late afternoon), so it avoids lions and hyenas that hunt more at night.
- Prey: gazelles (Thomson's, Grant's), impala, springbok, hares, young wildebeest, guineafowl. Not buffalo or adult zebra.
- Cheetahs do not roar; they chirp, purr, growl, hiss and "stutter bark" ⚑. Cubs chirp loudly to call the mother.
- They cannot climb well and have semi-retractable claws.

## 4. Threats (for the "world" system)

Sourced or widely reported, to confirm before use:
- Lions, hyenas and (for young cubs) eagles ⚑ take cubs; the main cause of cub deaths ([Hilborn et al. 2018](https://dx.doi.org/10.1007/s00265-018-2481-y)).
- Drought and prey shortage; habitat loss; roads and fences; conflict with farms ⚑.
- Humans can be a threat (poaching, trade of cubs as pets) and a help (conservancies, collars for research) ⚑. Handle gently for children: show the threat, not the violence.
- Disease; injury (a broken leg is the end for a hunter) ⚑.

## 5. Places (settings for the "world" system)

- Serengeti-Mara plains (open grass, huge herds, many lions).
- Kruger and similar bushveld (thicker cover, harder chases, success lower than open savanna).
- Namibian farmland (large home ranges; people and livestock). Home ranges of 241 to 849 km² were recorded in Botswana ([Home range study](https://bioone.org/journals/african-journal-of-wildlife-research/volume-39/issue-1/056.039.0102/Home-Range-Use-of-Free-Ranging-Cheetah-on-Farm-and/10.3957/056.039.0102.full)).
- Sahara and Iran (a very few animals remain; Asiatic cheetah is critically endangered) ⚑. A "rare and hard" scenario.
- Territory sizes differ widely by place: about 37 km² for Serengeti males against several hundred km² in Botswana ([Spatial behaviour](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/06/180628120049.htm)). Prey density decides it.
