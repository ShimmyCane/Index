# Reading list

These came from the conversation that started the project. The books have not been opened or checked. The one paper has been matched to its journal listing and abstract (2026-11-17, see `best_practices.md` section 4); its full text has not been read. Check each one before it is quoted in a piece.

For what research says today, with strength of evidence, see `best_practices.md`.

Books
- Erika Lee, *The Making of Asian America*: immigration, identity and why communities differ by generation.
- Monica Sone, *Nisei Daughter*: a memoir of growing up between a Japanese home and an American school.
- Iris Chang, *The Chinese in America*: a history of Chinese immigration and identity.
- Anne Fadiman, *The Spirit Catches You and You Fall Down*: how two cultural frames can fail to understand each other.

Reference
- Densho encyclopedia: Japanese American generational terms (Issei, Nisei, Sansei, Yonsei, Gosei, Kibei, Shin-Issei, Nikkei) and their history.
- The difference between 华侨, 华人 and 华裔 (citizen abroad, ethnic Chinese, of Chinese descent).

Paper
- Li, Y.-H. A. and Matthews, S. (2022), "Turning the Tide: Reversing Heritage Language Attrition and Shift in a Chinese American Family", *Journal of Chinese Linguistics* 50(2), 291 to 322. Abstract matches the claim: the arrival of a third generation moved second-generation parents to take the language up again. One family case study.
