# Our Family Compass — the framework

Version 0.2 (2026-11-17). A proposal to react to. Round 1 answers are in `convo/convo v0.1.md`. The rooms are a starting set: they live as data, will grow, and can be drawn differently over time.

## 1. The one idea

Not a set of instructions for living. A way for the children to find their way back to themselves.

Guiding line: roots deep enough to know where they came from, wings strong enough to explore, and a compass they can trust when nobody is there to say which way to go.

Who it is for: the children, the parents, one child, or the family. Each piece says which.

Two tests follow from it, and every later decision is checked against them:
- **Connected, not constrained.** A piece may offer heritage, family and values. It may not oblige the reader to repeat the parents' life.
- **Written with them, not for them.** The record has several authors: both parents, grandparents, mentors and, from the first year, the children. The start is mostly one voice (about 90% Jimmy, 10% Ying); the aim is an even share, or at least that everyone reads it and takes part. Nothing in the structure favours one author.

## 2. The palace

The framework is drawn as a building, because a building can be walked and a list cannot. The rooms hold parts of the compass, not facts to memorise.

| Place | What it is | What lives there |
|---|---|---|
| **Courtyard** (centre) | The compass itself | The one idea and the two tests |
| **Principles** (north) | What we hold in any era | Tensions held in balance, not rules |
| **Library** (east) | What we have learned | Stories, mistakes, traditions, people who shaped us |
| **Village** (south) | Trusted voices | Adults who see the world differently and disagree with respect |
| **Workshop** (west) | Judgment and practice | Dilemmas, scenarios, money, conflict, planning, reflection |
| **Gate** (the way in) | Four questions before building | Identity, success, morality, freedom |
| **Roots** (the ground) | Where the family came from | Generations, languages, the words for them |
| **Foundation** (under the floor) | Level 1 values | The few things that stay |
| **Open sky** (above) | Level 3 | What belongs to the children to choose |

A naming choice: the whole thing is the Compass, so the north room is called **Principles**. Two things with one name would confuse a child.

The three levels of values sit in the section of the building:
- **Foundation, level 1, foundational.** Dignity, kindness, honesty, curiosity. Stable.
- **Rooms, level 2, adaptive.** How to speak, work, collaborate and move through society. The rooms can be rearranged as the times change.
- **Open sky, level 3, personal.** Career, faith, way of life, which traditions to keep, ambitions. Increasingly theirs.

Sorting test for any value:
1. Would I still hold this if they chose a life unlike mine? If yes, it may be level 1.
2. Is it about how to do something well in this era? Level 2.
3. Is it my taste, or my path? Level 3. Say so on the piece.

## 3. The rooms in more depth

### Principles
Each principle is a pair in tension. The second half keeps the first half from going wrong.
- Kind, with boundaries.
- Curious, with judgment.
- Capable, and humble.
- Independent, and in relationship.
- Ambitious, without measuring worth by achievement.
- Aware that everyone is fighting a battle you may not see.

Work to do here: find the tensions that are missing, mark the ones the two parents weigh differently, and move anything that is really a preference up to the open sky.

### Library
Family journeys, grandparents' lives, traditions and why they are kept, personal mistakes, work, and the people who shaped the parents. A story is told once in the teller's own voice, then kept.

The Library is the most private room. Its content never sits in this public folder.

### Village
Parents, grandparents, teachers, coaches, mentors, friends, extended family, and people whose lives are very different. A parent should not be the only authority on how to live. The children gain from seeing good adults disagree respectfully, make mistakes and take other paths.

Work to do here: name the first five people; choose the one question each is asked; decide how a person with no interest in technology adds a voice (a recording made on a visit is enough); ask each person's permission.

### Workshop
The children practise deciding. A workshop piece gives a situation and no answer, then five standing questions:
1. What are my responsibilities here?
2. What might the other person be going through?
3. What could happen next, for each choice?
4. What would a kind and courageous response look like?
5. What if my first judgment was wrong?

The Workshop also separates four words that are easy to blur: being **good**, being **nice**, being **strong**, being **wise**.

### Gate
Four questions to answer before any value is written down. Both parents answer alone, then compare.
- **Identity.** What makes you who you are, apart from nationality, profession, wealth and family?
- **Success.** What does a successful life look like without money, grades and status?
- **Morality.** How should the children tell apart being good, being nice, being strong and being wise?
- **Freedom.** What should parents teach, and what should they deliberately leave to be discovered?

### Roots
Where the family came from, and the words people use for generations. Japanese American communities named each generation (Issei, Nisei, Sansei, Yonsei), because a shared history made each generation's experience distinct. Chinese has the numbers (第一代, 第二代, 一代半) and separate words for citizenship, ethnicity and descent (华侨, 华人, 华裔), and a far more varied history behind them. The number alone says little; the story behind it says more.

One idea to hold on to, still to be checked in its source: sometimes the arrival of grandchildren leads the parents back to their heritage. Roots can grow in both directions.

Rule for this room: roots are offered, never graded.

## 4. Pieces

The end products are small. A child meets one piece at a time.

| Kind | Size | Example of a title |
|---|---|---|
| **Story**, with narration | 1 to 3 minutes | The time I learned that being right is not the same as being wise |
| **Field note** | one principle | Listen twice before answering once |
| **Open question** | one question, no answer | When is it right to break a promise? |
| **Decision guide** | 3 to 5 questions | Is this person difficult, or dangerous? |
| **Moral game** | a scenario with choices and consequences | The friend who cheated |
| **Small reading** | one page | A paragraph from a book, with one question |
| **The hammer** | one idea, said straight, no story | What a boundary is |
| **Archive item** | one object | A letter, a photograph, a recording |

### Anatomy of a piece
Every piece has a front and a back.

**Front (what the child meets):** a title, a hook, a picture or a voice, and one thing to do. Fun and easy to approach.

**Back (what the author writes down first):**
| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Objective | One sentence: after this, the child can… |
| Lesson | The idea, said plainly, for the adult reader |
| Room | Principles, Library, Village, Workshop or Roots |
| Level | 1, 2 or 3 |
| Values | Which principles it touches |
| For | One child, the children, the parents or the whole family, and the age it suits now |
| Kind | From the table above |
| Author and date | Who wrote it, and when |
| Voice | An optional recording linked to the piece |
| Revises | The earlier piece it changes, if any |
| Ending | The question or choice it leaves open |
| What it is not | The limit of the lesson |

Worked example (a moral game):
- Front: "Your friend copied the answers. Nobody else saw. What now?" Three paths to try, each with what happens next.
- Objective: the child can name two responsibilities that pull in different directions, and say one kind and courageous thing.
- Lesson: loyalty and honesty can conflict; courage can be gentle.
- Room: Workshop. Level: 1 (honesty), with a level 2 skill (how to say a hard thing).
- Ending: "What if you were wrong about what you saw?"
- What it is not: a rule that you must always report a friend.

## 5. Two layers

Voices: writing is enough, and a voice note can be linked to any thought or piece (recorded on the page from ⚙️, or typed in from something a grandparent or mentor emailed or texted). Stored in a private bucket.


- **The knowledge layer.** A structured record of people, stories, values, lessons and the links between them. Orderly, searchable, private. The palace itself (rooms, places on the map, connections) is data, kept as JSON in the family database, so it can grow and be redrawn without changing the page.
- **The experience layer.** A palace the children walk through: they wander into a room, read, hear a story, meet a trusted voice, play a mini game and interact with an idea. A child never needs to see the whole philosophy.

This round builds the first sketch of the knowledge layer's map. The experience layer comes after the framework holds.

## 6. What could go wrong, and the rule that guards against it

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| It turns into a sermon | Every piece ends with a question or a choice, never only a moral |
| One voice | A room opens to the children only when it holds pieces from at least two authors |
| A preference dressed as a principle | Every piece carries a level. Level 1 stays short (proposal: seven at most) |
| Frozen in time | Every piece has an author and a date. A change of mind is a new piece that points to the old one; the old one stays |
| Private stories in a public place | The repo holds structure only. Content lives in the family database or a `private/` folder |
| Too long to read | A size limit per kind (a story is 1 to 3 minutes) |
| Different readers | Each piece says who it is for: one child, the children, the parents or the family |
| Children as audience only | Their answers and disagreements are kept as pieces from the first year |
| Heritage as obligation | Roots pieces offer and never test |
| The framework never ends | Three test pieces are made early (round 4), so the framework is tested by use |

## 7. The path

1. **Stand at the Gate.** Both parents answer the four questions, alone, then compare.
2. **Lay the Foundation.** Sort values into the three levels.
3. **Call the Village.** Name the first voices and ask each one question.
4. **Make three test pieces.** One story with narration, one open question, one moral game. Try them with the children.
5. **Open the rooms.** Build the children's side, the database and the recordings.
6. **Hand over the pen.** The children add pieces. Revisions are kept with their dates.

## 8. What research adds

See `research/best_practices.md`. The main suggestions: put hard times and how they were met into the Library; let the children play moral games together before a parent is in the room; keep level 1 short, explain level 2, leave level 3 open; say what ranks above achievement; do not promise measurable results from the Workshop.

## 9. What other nursery projects already give this one

- **Adventure:** recorded narration kept in a private store, a family sign-in, and a story told in chapters. The same route serves a parent's narrated story.
- **Better Parenting:** scenario cards with a situation, what is going on and what to say; answer first, then compare. The Workshop's moral games can take this shape, with the answer left open.
- **Felix Gear Wars:** one story in three reading tiers. One objective with two fronts for two ages follows the same idea.
- **Feedback notes and the ⚙️ panel:** already in this page.
- **AI copy-and-paste loop** (`02 Refs/Skills/ai_json_loop.md`): a way to draft a piece's back with an AI chat while the page holds no key.
