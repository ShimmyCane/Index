## 1. The world in one breath

Far away, in a different place and a different time, lies **Ondra**. At its centre stands **Vael, the World Tree**, so tall its crown is lost in cloud. In its roots, people wake with no memory. Around it lies an old forest, a small village, and the ruins of a village older than anyone can count. Beyond the forest: a desert that was once a forest, lakes that were once a single night of tears, cities of gold domes and iron smoke, grass seas, stair-step mountains and a northern sky that burns green.

The world has been at peace for three hundred years. The peace is tired. Under it run old wrongs that nobody talks about, new wrongs that people pretend not to see, and a secret about something the whole world was made to forget.

Soon the Tree will bloom. It blooms once in a thousand years. When it does, everything forgotten comes back. That is **the Reckoning**. It will turn the world upside down: peasants will become heroes, and kings will kneel.

For now, a child wakes in the moss, and does not know their own name.

## 2. What each people calls the world

Nobody agrees, which tells you a lot.

| People | Their word | What it means to them |
|---|---|---|
| Mossbrook and the forest folk | **the Green** | The world is a garden you borrow and give back |
| Tallenai of the desert | **the Given** | Everything is a gift, especially water, and gifts make debts |
| Lumai of the lakes | **the Mirror** | The world below the water is the true one; ours is its reflection |
| The Court of Solvane | **the Realm** | Everything under the Sun belongs to the Sun Throne |
| Ferrow | **the Market** | What has a price can be shared; what has no price gets wasted |
| The Rootkin (forgotten) | a long low hum | Untranslatable. Something like *the-place-that-is-still-growing* |

Scholars, when they want to sound neutral, say **Ondra**, an old word from the Age of Roots that means *the round of things*.

## 3. The myth of the beginning

This is how Granny Wren tells it, and how most people in the Green believe it. Other peoples tell it differently (see `peoples.md`).

> **Brother Sun and Sister Moon** loved each other more than anything, but they could never be in the sky together. When he rose, she had to set. When she rose, he had to sleep.
>
> So they made a gift for each other, to hold between them. The Sun gave warmth. The Moon gave a tear. Where the warmth and the tear met in the dark earth, a seed woke.
>
> The seed became **Vael**. Its roots drank from the deep water under the world. Its crown held up the sky, so the Sun and the Moon could pass each other gently, day after night after day, for always.
>
> When the Tree put out its first seven leaves, each leaf sang, and each song became a great spirit to keep one part of the world. These are **the Wardens**. And when the Tree put out its first seeds, the seeds that fell in its roots became the **Rootkin**, the first people, small and patient, who tended the Tree.
>
> Later, from the edges of the world, came the **Walkers**: tall, quick, curious, forgetful. Humans. The Rootkin taught them the first and only law.

**The Giving Law** (also called the Pact of the Tree):

> *Take with one hand. Give with the other.*

Every people of Ondra still knows some version of it. Few keep it.

## 4. The Ages

Time in Ondra is counted in **Rings**, like a tree. The Keepers of the Root keep a great book, the **Ring-Book**, with one page for each year. The present year is written at the top of every page as *the 3,112th Ring*. Ordinary people just count from the Concord: *the 300th summer of the Concord*.

| Age | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The Age of Song** | Before counting | The Tree grows. The Wardens are sung. The Rootkin wake. The Walkers arrive. |
| **The Age of Roots** | Rings 1 – about 1,900 | The Giving Law. **Aun**, the first village, grows inside the Tree's roots, where Rootkin and humans live together. The land east of the forest, the **Greenreach**, is a land of rivers and huge forests guarded by the River Serpent. People spread to the lakes' valley (then dry), the grasslands, the mountains and the coast. **The first bloom** of Vael happens near the start of this age; it is when the Giving Law was spoken. |
| **The Age of Crowns** | about 1,900 – 2,700 | The Sunward Empire rises at **Solvane**. Roads, aqueducts, libraries, the scholars' examinations: a golden time for some. Then **the Glass Kings**: emperors who learned to catch sunlight in glass. Sun-glass lit cities and drove machines, but making it needed sand and fire. They burned the forests of the Greenreach, then ground the ash and the riverbeds into sand. The River Serpent sickened and became the Salt Serpent. The Greenreach became **the Ashen Reach**, a desert. The Rootkin refused to give the Glass Kings the Tree's heartwood. |
| **The Great Forgetting** | Ring 2,694 | **Orrin the Bright**, the last Glass King, bound the spirit of forgetting, the Hush, and turned it on the Rootkin. In a single night the whole world forgot they had ever existed. Their names fell out of every book. In Ondra, a thing that nobody remembers begins to fade. The Rootkin faded. Aun emptied. |
| **The Night Without Moon** | Ring 2,700 | Sister Moon grieved for the Rootkin and did not rise. For one night the sky was black. Her tears fell for that whole night and filled the low valley north of the forest. That is the **Mirrorlakes**. They drowned half of Solvane. The Glass Kings fell. Orrin vanished. |
| **The Breaking** | 2,700 – 2,812 | A hundred years of small wars over the pieces of the Empire. Glass-slaves escape and found **Ferrow**. Desert families become the **Caravan Houses**. Lake survivors build floating islands and become the **Lumai**. The grass clans ride free. |
| **The Concord** | Ring 2,812 | Five powers sign **the Concord of Five Waters** on the Lantern Isle in the middle of the lakes: Solvane, Ferrow, the Caravan Houses, the Lantern Synod and the Tallgrass Clans. No war between them. The Forest of the Tree is neutral ground: *no army walks under Vael*. It is renewed every hundred years. |
| **The Age of Embers** | Now, Ring 3,112 | Three hundred years of peace. The Concord's third renewal is due this year, and nobody is sure it will be signed. The desert wells are failing. Ferrow wants the forest's ironwood. Solvane has an eleven-year-old queen and a regent who smiles too much. And the Tree has put out a bud. |

## 5. The Hush, and why you have forgotten

**The Hush** is a spirit, the eighth one, the one the songs leave out. It is not evil. It is the spirit of rest and forgetting: it lets a wound stop hurting, a quarrel fade, a sad day blur so you can sleep. Without it, every bad memory would stay sharp forever.

The Glass Kings caught it and used it as a weapon. Since then, it has been wounded and wild. It drifts through the world like soft grey snow with moths in it. Where it passes, people forget small things: where they put a spoon, the name of a song, why they were angry. In some places it settles and stays. A few people who catch it fully forget everything.

**The Seedlings.** Sometimes, over the centuries, someone was dying, or lost, or in terrible danger near the Tree, and Vael took them in. It laid them to sleep in its roots, wrapped in moss, out of time. The Hush took their memories while they slept. When the Tree needs hands, it wakes one. Villagers call them **Seedlings**, or *the Tree's children*. The linden bells in Mossbrook ring by themselves when one wakes. It has not happened in living memory, except once, when Granny Wren was a girl.

The player is a Seedling. They wake with:
- no name, no memory, no home;
- a small humming key with a sun on one side and a moon on the other;
- a scruffy grey dog who has guarded their sleep for longer than anyone knows (**Bristle**);
- and the strange ability to see things the world has forgotten, like the faint shapes of Rootkin.

**Who were they before?** The storyteller does not decide alone. The family's choices point to an answer, chapter by chapter. Possible truths (storyteller only; pick one when the story has earned it, or mix):
1. A Rootkin-friend from the Age of Roots, one of the last humans who lived in Aun, put to sleep by the Rootkin to carry their memory forward.
2. An apprentice of the Glass Kings who refused to help with the Great Forgetting and ran into the forest with the Sun Key.
3. A child from the Night Without Moon, swept out of drowning Solvane and carried to the Tree by the Great Heron.
4. Someone from *after* the Reckoning, sent back by the Tree because something went wrong. (Book 3 or later. Do not use early.)

Truths are revealed in small pieces: a smell that feels like home, a song you half know, a word in a dead language you can read. Never all at once.

## 6. The Twin Keys

The Tree's heart, deep in its trunk, has a door with two locks: one for the Sun, one for the Moon. The **Sun Key** and the **Moon Key** were made in the Age of Roots so that nobody alone could open the heart, where the Tree keeps everything it remembers.

- **The key in the player's hand** shows both a sun and a moon. Is it the Sun Key? Both keys fused? A copy? (Storyteller decides as the story earns it. Default: it is the Sun Key, and its moon side shows it longs for its twin.) It hums near Rootkin things and near the Tree, and goes cold near nightbloom.
- **The Moon Key** is hidden by the Lantern Synod in the Mirrorlakes, out of old shame.
- **The Ash Hand** wants both keys, because the heart also holds the sun-fire the Glass Kings sealed away.

This links to the family's earlier car-ride story: the Sun and Moon Chest, its humming key, Bristle and the Old Lady. In Ondra, the chest is in the ruins of Aun (`book1.md`, chapter 4).

## 7. The Reckoning

When Vael blooms, once in a thousand years, the Tree *remembers everything at once*. Its memory floods back into the world.

What that means:
- **The Hush breaks.** Everything forgotten returns: the Rootkin step out of the air; names come back into books; old songs are suddenly known by everyone.
- **Every promise comes due.** Promises made under the Tree (and the Concord was signed under a cutting of it on the Lantern Isle) bind again. Those who kept the Giving Law are lifted; those who broke it are brought low. This is how kings end up on their knees.
- **The sky turns over.** For a day, the Sun and the Moon stand in the sky together. The Lumai call it the Wedding; Solvane's priests call it the End.
- **Old dangers wake too.** The sun-fire in the Tree's heart, the Glass Golems in the desert, the Unremembered, and whatever is left of Orrin the Bright.
- **It can go two ways.** If the Tree's heart is opened with both keys by people who keep the Giving Law, the bloom heals: the Ashen Reach greens, the Salt Serpent remembers it was a river. If it is opened by force, or with greed, the sun-fire spills out, and the world burns.

The Reckoning is not the end of the game. It is the turning point. There is a world before it and a world after it, and lives can be played in both.

**When?** The **Bloom Clock** (section 9) counts down. Book 1 sees the first bud. The Reckoning should not come before Book 4 or 5 unless Jimmy wants it sooner.

## 8. How dark it gets, and how it is told

The world is made for young children to explore, and to grow up in. It does not pretend the world is always kind. It tells hard truths gently.

- **Terrible things are told, not shown.** War, cruelty and loss happen in the past, off stage, or far away. They reach the children as an elder's story, a ruin, a sad song, an empty chair.
- **Injustice is shown by what it does to ordinary people.** A girl who works the bellows to pay her father's debt. A herder who must pay for water. A tree with a red mark. The children can always do something small that helps.
- **Treachery is real, and can be noticed.** People lie. The game rewards noticing: a smell, a story that does not fit, a dog who growls at a smiling stranger.
- **Poison puts people to sleep, it does not kill on the page.** Nightbloom brings the "grey sleep", and there is always a cure to search for.
- **Nobody is only good or only bad.** Ferrow built schools. Solvane built libraries. The Lantern Synod is kind and frightened. Even the Ash Hand began with people who were hurt.
- **Bravery, cleverness, kindness and loyalty** each open different doors. A wrong answer brings a hint, never a dead end. (`storyteller.md`)
- **Every chapter ends with a question.** What the family chooses changes the world.

As the children grow, Jimmy can turn the dial up: later books and other modes can show more.

## 9. How the world lives and changes

The goal: a world that breathes, that changes because of what the players do, where many lives at different times are woven back and forth.

### Leaves and Rings
- A **Leaf** is one life a player plays. The first leaf is the Seedling who wakes in Book 1.
- A **Ring** is a time in the world's history that can be played: the Age of Roots (Aun's best days), the Age of Crowns (the burning of the Greenreach), the Night Without Moon, the Age of Embers (now), and After the Reckoning.
- A player can have many leaves in different rings. Later modes might let a family play a Rootkin child in the Age of Roots one week and the Seedling the next.

### Echoes
What happens in an earlier ring **echoes** into later ones. Plant a seed in the Age of Roots, and there is a grove in the Age of Embers. Keep a promise to a desert family in the Age of Crowns, and their descendants greet you as kin. Betray someone, and a place is named after the betrayal.

The storyteller writes each echo as a `history` entry with **[echo]** in the title, and checks echoes before writing a chapter set in a later ring.

### Clocks
Between chapters, the world moves on its own. Each **clock** has six segments. The storyteller ticks clocks forward (or back) at each run, by what the players did and what the factions would do.

| Clock | Starts at | When it fills |
|---|---|---|
| **Bloom** (to the Reckoning) | 1 / 6 (a bud) | The Tree blooms |
| **War** (Solvane and Ferrow) | 2 / 6 | The Concord breaks; armies march |
| **Red Marks** (Ferrow's cutting reaches the forest) | 1 / 6 | Woodcutters come under Vael |
| **Thirst** (the desert wells) | 3 / 6 | The Caravan Houses turn on each other |
| **Ash Hand** (the secret plot) | 1 / 6 | They make their move for the keys |
| **Hush** (forgetting spreading) | 2 / 6 | A whole town forgets itself |

Clocks are stored as one `setting` entry ("World clocks") so Jimmy can see and change them under the hood.

### Standing
How each people, faction and spirit thinks of the player: **stranger → known → trusted → kin**, or **wary → hostile**. Kept in the journal (`adv_notes`) on the faction's or spirit's entry.

### Seasons
Each chapter moves time forward a little. The year: **Thaw**, **Bloom**, **High Sun**, **Harvest**, **Long Dark**. Book 1 begins in late Thaw. Festivals are in `peoples.md`.

## 10. Inspiration, and care

Ondra borrows from cultures across our world, with respect. It takes *ideas* (a way of keeping memory, a way of building, a way of sharing water), not sacred things, and it does not copy any one people. Names are invented.

| In Ondra | Inspired by |
|---|---|
| Vael, the World Tree | World trees in many traditions: the Norse ash, the Mesoamerican ceiba, the Baltic tree of the sun, the Siberian shaman's tree |
| The Giving Law | Reciprocity in many cultures, especially the Andean idea of *ayni* (I help you, you help me) |
| Mossbrook's hearth spirits and forest customs | Slavic and Baltic village life (house spirits, linden trees, name days), Japanese *satoyama* (living with the edge of the forest) |
| Rootkin living houses | The living root bridges grown by the Khasi and Jaintia peoples of Meghalaya, India |
| The Tallenai caravans | Saharan salt caravans, the manuscript libraries of Timbuktu, Persian *qanats* and windcatchers, desert guest-right |
| Rememberers | West African griots, keepers of history in song |
| The Lumai | The leg-rowers and floating gardens of Inle Lake (Myanmar), the reed islands of Lake Titicaca, the *chinampas* of Tenochtitlan, lantern festivals across Asia |
| Solvane | Byzantine Constantinople, Tang-dynasty Chang'an and its examinations, Rome's roads and aqueducts |
| Ferrow | The Hanseatic League, the Venetian Arsenal, the early industrial towns |
| The Tallgrass Clans | The peoples of the Central Asian steppe: felt tents, eagle hunters, horse culture |
| The Yanari knot-records | The Andean *khipu* (knotted cord records) and mountain terraces; prayer flags of the Himalaya |
| The Wayfinders | Pacific Islander wayfinding by stars and swells, Marshallese stick charts |
| The Velde | Herding peoples of the far north |
| The Whisperfox, the Stillhorse, Grandfather Moss | Fox spirits of East Asia, the water horses of Northern Europe, the Slavic forest lord |

The rule for art (from Jimmy's preferences): when the game borrows a look, it borrows from historical artists, never living ones.
