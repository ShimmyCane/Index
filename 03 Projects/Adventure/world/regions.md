# Ondra — Regions

From the moss where you wake, outward to the edges of the map. Each region has: what it looks, sounds and smells like; its history; places; customs; and story hooks.

```
                     ❄️ THE WHITE NORTH (the Velde)
                               │
   🐎 TALLGRASS ──────── 🏮 THE MIRRORLAKES ─────── 🏰 SOLVANE (gold domes, half drowned)
      (the clans)          (Velomar, Lantern Isle)         │
          │                        │                       │ the Sun Road
          │               🌳 THE GREENHOLLOW               │
          │          (Vael · Mossbrook · Aun)  ───────── ⚒️ FERROW (iron and canals)
          │                        │                       │
   ⛰️ STAIRSTEP MOUNTAINS    🏜️ THE ASHEN REACH ──────── 🌊 THE SCATTERED ISLES
       (the Yanari)       (Ibbasar, the Glass Flats)        (the Wayfinders)
```

Distances, for a child's sense of the world: Mossbrook is an hour's walk from the Waking Hollow. The lakes are three days north by cart. Solvane is a week. The desert's edge is four days east, and Ibbasar is ten days into the sand by caravan.

## 1. The Greenhollow — the forest of the Tree

**Look, sound, smell.** Ferns taller than a grown-up. Light in long gold ribbons. Moss on everything, even the moss. Honey and wet earth, linden blossom in Bloom season. Woodpeckers, a stream that never freezes, and a wind in the high leaves that sounds like something very large breathing in its sleep.

**History.** The oldest forest in Ondra, and the only one the Glass Kings never dared to cut: Vael frightened them. By the Concord it is neutral ground: *no army walks under Vael*. The **Keepers of the Root** watch over it, though there are only a handful left.

**Places**
- **The Waking Hollow.** A bowl of moss inside a great root, where Seedlings sleep. Where Book 1 begins. Heart-shaped linden-like leaves drift here even in winter.
- **Vael, the World Tree.** See its own section below.
- **Mossbrook.** The small living village. See below.
- **Aun, the Old Village.** The ruins in the roots. See below.
- **The Carved Stones.** A line of grey standing stones running west from the Hollow to Aun, each carved with the **seed-eye**: a circle with a dot in the middle. The Rootkin's sign. Villagers say it means "watching"; really it means "growing".
- **The Ironwood Stand.** South-east, near the forest's edge: trees with dark wood as hard as iron. Only grows within a day of Vael. Ferrow wants it. This is where the first **red marks** appear.
- **The Singing Stream.** Runs from the Tree's roots to Mossbrook. Its water tastes faintly of honey and helps you remember small things (a faint pull against the Hush).
- **The Mirefen.** A boggy dell where mire-lights drift at night. Rude travellers get lost; polite ones get home.
- **The Hollow Oak of Grandmother Owl.** A dead oak, hollow as a tower, home of the old grey owl who always asks "Who?"

**Customs.** Never take the first mushroom or the last. Say thank you aloud when you drink from the stream. A stranger in the forest is offered bread and asked nothing for a day.

**Hooks.** Red marks on the ironwood. A sick mossback deer that has eaten nightbloom. Rootkin glimmers that only the Seedling can see. A Keeper's grey cloak found empty on a branch.

### Vael, the World Tree

So big that from its roots it looks like a cliff. Its bark is in ridges as tall as a house. Its roots are as wide as roads and run under the whole forest. Its crown is lost in cloud; on clear winter nights the White North's green lights seem to hang in its branches.

- **The Listening Root.** A root that rises out of the ground like a bridge. Put your ear to it and you hear what the Tree is "thinking": wind, water, and sometimes voices from other times (other players' lives, see Leaves and Rings in `bible.md`).
- **The Rings.** Where a great limb fell long ago, its cut face shows rings. Touch a ring, and you might glimpse that year.
- **The Heart Door.** Somewhere inside the trunk, a door with a sun lock and a moon lock. Nobody living has seen it.
- **The Bud.** High in the crown, at the start of Book 1, a single bud as big as a house, pale gold, appears. Only the Seedling and Bristle notice at first.

### Mossbrook — the small village

**Look.** Twenty-two houses of grey stone and dark timber along the Singing Stream, roofs thick with moss and flowers. Over every door, a carving of the sun and the moon, and many have the seed-eye too, though nobody remembers why. A green with a great linden tree hung with small bronze bells: **the linden bells**. A mill, a smithy, a bee-garden, a bakehouse whose bread is famous for three valleys.

**Sound and smell.** Bees. The mill wheel. Wood smoke and bread. Bells when the wind is right, and on the morning of Book 1, bells ringing by themselves.

**History.** Founded about four hundred years ago by families who fled the Breaking. They chose the forest because no army would come there. They built with stones taken from the edge of Aun, which is why their doors carry Rootkin signs they cannot read. There is something sad in the village's past that nobody likes to talk about: at the Breaking, Mossbrook turned away a boat of refugees from drowning Solvane, and the boat was lost. The village still leaves a candle in the window on the Night Without Moon, and does not say why.

**Customs.**
- **The hearth bowl.** Every house puts out a bowl of milk and a crust for its hearthwisp. Quarrels make the hearthwisp sulk, and then the bread won't rise.
- **The Naming Bowl.** A child, or a newcomer who wants to belong, chooses a name at the Singing Stream: they say it over a wooden bowl of stream water, and if the water stays still, the name is theirs. (Chapter 2 or 3 is the Seedling's naming.)
- **Linden Day** in Bloom season: everyone ties a ribbon to the linden for something they hope for.
- **Market Day** each new moon, when traders come through on the Sun Road.

**People.** Granny Wren, Headwoman Marla Ash, Tobin the miller's son, Nettle the bee-girl, Old Pell the woodcutter, Smith Hadda (see `peoples.md`).

**Hooks.** The bells ringing by themselves. A sulking hearthwisp. Old Pell's secret. The candle in the window. A stranger at Market Day asking too many questions about the Tree.

### Aun — the Old Village

**Look.** Half swallowed by roots, half grown *out of* them. Houses that are not built but grown: walls of living root woven like baskets, bridges of root over dry streams, doorways shaped like the seed-eye. All empty. Everything green-gold and still. It feels like a room someone has just left.

**History.** The first village in the world, from the Age of Roots, where Rootkin and humans lived together and grew houses instead of building them. It emptied in a single night at the Great Forgetting. People since have avoided it without knowing why: the Hush lies thick there.

**Places inside.**
- **The Root Gate.** The way in. It opens only to someone who says thank you to it (or to the key).
- **The Grown Hall.** A great round room with a chest at its centre carved with suns, moons and seed-eyes: **the Sun and Moon Chest**. (Links to the family's earlier crypt story.)
- **The Wall of Hands.** Hundreds of small hand-prints pressed into the living wood. Rootkin hands, and a few bigger human ones.
- **The Dry Well.** Goes down to the Underlake, the deep water under the world.

**Hooks.** The key hums louder here. Rootkin glimmers become almost solid. The Seedling can read a few of the carvings without knowing how.

## 2. The Mirrorlakes — the Moon's tears

**Look.** Nine wide lakes joined by channels, so still that the sky lies in them twice. Reed beds, stilt houses, floating gardens of tomatoes and lilies anchored with poles. Paper lanterns at night, thousands, on the water. Mist every morning.

**Sound and smell.** Oars. Herons. Wind in the reeds. Lantern oil and lotus.

**History.** A dry valley until the Night Without Moon, when Sister Moon's tears filled it in a single night. The towns there drowned; their towers still stand under the water, and on calm days you can see the roofs. Survivors tied reed rafts together and never went back to land. They became the **Lumai**.

**Places.**
- **Velomar, the Floating City.** Nine islands of reed and timber joined by bridges, moored over the drowned city of Old Velo. Lantern halls, floating markets, the Synod's Moonhouse.
- **The Lantern Isle.** The one rocky island in the middle of the largest lake. A cutting of Vael grows there, planted when the Concord was signed under it. The Concord is renewed here this year.
- **The Drowned Towers.** Roofs and spires below the surface. Divers bring up old glass.
- **The Outreeds.** Where the Unlanterned outcasts live on rafts at the lakes' edges.
- **The Heron Stones.** Tall rocks where the Great Heron is sometimes seen at dawn.

**Customs.** Lantern Night (see `peoples.md`). Leg-rowing: standing on one leg at the stern, the other leg wrapped round the oar, so both hands are free for nets. Never throw anything into the lakes without saying sorry to the Moon.

**Hooks.** A pilgrim in Mossbrook carrying a mirror carp in a jar. The Moon Key. The Synod's old shame: their ancestors made mirrors for the Glass Kings.

## 3. The Ashen Reach — the desert that was a forest

**Look.** Red and gold dunes, salt flats white as snow, black glassy plains. Sometimes, in a wind-cut, the stumps of trees turned to stone, standing in rows: the ghost of the Greenreach. Oases ringed with date palms. Windtowers on mud-brick cities. A sky so full of stars at night it looks like spilled sugar.

**Sound and smell.** Dunes that sing (a deep hum when the sand slides). Camel bells. Mint tea. Hot stone and dust.

**History.** Once **the Greenreach**: rivers, forests, the River Serpent. The Glass Kings burned it for sun-glass and ground the land into sand. The River Serpent sank under the dunes and became the Salt Serpent. The desert peoples remember the green in song, which the Hush could not entirely erase.

**Places.**
- **Ibbasar, the Library Oasis.** A mud-brick city of windtowers around a spring. The **Hall of Ten Thousand Pages**, where the Rememberers write down what they sing, and the Well Court.
- **The Glass Flats.** A plain of black fused sand where the Glass Kings' furnaces stood. Sleeping Glass Golems lie half buried. Nightbloom grows in the cracks.
- **The Stone Forest.** Petrified trees in rows.
- **The Seven Wells.** The great underground channels (qanats) that bring mountain water under the sand. Four of the seven are failing.
- **The Dune of the Serpent.** The largest dune; its song is the Salt Serpent snoring.

**Hooks.** A Rememberer comes to Mossbrook with the Song of the Green That Was. Water sold by Well-Lords. A glasswight's bargain. Ferrow buying desert sand for glass, the same mistake again.

## 4. Solvane — the City of Gold Domes

**Look.** A great city on a river delta: gold domes, white towers, aqueducts on arches, a harbour of painted ships. The old lower city, the **Drowned Quarter**, lies under the edge of the lake since the Night Without Moon; children dive there for old glass and coins. Banners of the sun everywhere.

**Sound and smell.** Temple bells, market criers, the court's trumpets. Incense, spices, fish, river mud.

**History.** Capital of the Sunward Empire and the Glass Kings. Broken by the Night Without Moon, it rebuilt smaller and prouder. The Sun Throne still claims, on paper, everything under the sun.

**Places.** The **Palace of a Thousand Steps** (rank is counted in which step you may stand on). The **Examination Halls**, where anyone may sit the scholar's test (if they can pay the fee). The **Ward-House**, where the Ward-Children live. The **Drowned Quarter** and its hidden **Glass Remnant**. The **Sun Road**, the old imperial road that runs from Solvane through Mossbrook to Ferrow.

**Hooks.** An eleven-year-old queen who wants a friend. A regent with a ring that smells faintly of nightbloom. Ward-Children.

## 5. Ferrow — the City of Iron and Canals

**Look.** Canals instead of streets, black with soot. Foundry chimneys. Cranes. Bridges that lift. A printing house, a great clock tower, guild halls with painted fronts. Some streets are rich and clean; some are not.

**Sound and smell.** Hammers day and night. The great bellows. Coal smoke, hot metal, ink, river water.

**History.** Founded by escaped glass-slaves during the Breaking, who swore *"No crown over us."* They became the best smiths in Ondra, then the best bankers. Now the Compact of Guilds rules, and the city is full of people who owe it money.

**Places.** The **Arsenal** (shipyard). The **Bellows Halls** where debt-children work. The **Free School** (real and good, for guild children). The **Counting House**. The **Founders' Gate**, still carved with *No crown over us*.

**Hooks.** Surveyors with red paint. A bellows-girl who runs away to the forest. Ferrow's new ships need ironwood keels.

---

## 6. The Tallgrass — the sea of grass

**Look.** Grass to the horizon, silver when the wind moves it. Round felt tents, horse herds, eagles on gloves. Burial mounds with standing stones. Since recently, Ferrow's fences.

**History.** The clans have ridden here since the Age of Roots. The Glass Kings never conquered them. Their treaty with Solvane gave them the grass forever. Ferrow, which did not sign that treaty, has started fencing it for sheep and roads.

**Hooks.** A clan rider arrives in Mossbrook on a lame horse with news of a fence war. The Wind Mare's foal.

---

## 7. The Stairstep Mountains — the Yanari

**Look.** Mountains carved into terraces like giant staircases, potatoes and quinoa-like grain on every step. Prayer-ribbons on the passes. Monasteries cut into the rock. Bridges of woven grass over gorges.

**History.** Their records are knotted cords, read by touch; the Hush cannot take what the hands remember. So the Yanari remember the Rootkin, faintly, as "the small growers" in their cords.

**Hooks.** A knot-keeper who can "read" the Seedling's past by touch. The Stone Bear, a mountain that sometimes rolls over in its sleep.

## 8. The far edges

- **The White North (the Velde).** Snow plains, reindeer herds, sky lights in green and violet that the Velde say are the Tree's crown catching the sun. Herders sing songs that *are* a person, not songs about a person. The Aurora Owl lives there.
- **The Scattered Isles (the Wayfinders).** Islands beyond the eastern coast. Sailors who cross the sea by the stars, the swells and the birds, with charts made of sticks and shells. They say the world is round, and that there is land on the other side where another tree grows. (A hook for much later.)
- **The Underlake.** The deep water under the world, where Vael's roots drink. Reached through the Dry Well in Aun, and, the Lumai say, through the bottom of the deepest lake. The Moon's Children may still live there.
