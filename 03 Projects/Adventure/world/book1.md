# Book 1 — The Waking

**Who:** the Seedling (the player, with no name yet), Bristle, Granny Wren, and the Storyteller. New friends join along the way: Tobin, Nettle, Sela, Wick.
**Where:** the Greenhollow only: the Waking Hollow, Mossbrook, Vael, Aun, the Ironwood Stand.
**When:** late Thaw into Bloom season, Ring 3,112, the 300th summer of the Concord.
**Length:** 8 chapters, each with a real ending and a question.

**What Book 1 is about.** Learning who you are by how you act. The player has no past, so every choice makes them. The first chapters are about exploring, noticing, being kind or brave or clever, and starting to hear about the wider world through the people who pass through. Nothing big is decided yet. The world rumbles in the distance.

**Themes.** Who am I? (A name is chosen, not given.) Belonging. Trust between the Seedling and Bristle. Noticing what others miss. Small injustices that ordinary people can push against. Remembering the forgotten.

**What the storyteller should learn about the player.** By the end of Book 1, the storyteller should have noticed whether this player tends to be **brave**, **clever**, **kind** or **loyal** (or a mix), and what they are curious about. Write it in the Seedling's journal. It shapes the Book 2 road and the first piece of their true past.

**Clocks at the start.** Bloom 1, War 2, Red Marks 1, Thirst 3, Ash Hand 1, Hush 2.

## Chapter 1 — Who? Who?
*Written: see `../story/b1c1.js` (voiced) and `supabase/002_seed.sql`.*

You wake on moss in the roots of the biggest tree in the world, with no memory, a humming sun-and-moon key in your hand, and a scruffy grey dog watching you. A whisperfox repeats your words. An owl asks "Who?" Bells ring to the east; carved stones lead west; the Tree rises above. A tiny bark-skinned figure on a stone puts a finger to its lips and vanishes.

**Ends on:** What is your name (say one, or ask the forest)? East to the bells, west along the stones, or up into the roots?

**Branches.**
- *A name is spoken:* the owl repeats it, satisfied, and it becomes the Seedling's working name (made official at the Naming Bowl in chapter 2 or 3).
- *Ask the forest:* Grandmother Owl offers a riddle, and its answer becomes the name.
- *East* → chapter 2 as planned. *West* → chapter 4's Aun comes earlier, briefly, then Bristle leads them to Mossbrook. *Up* → chapter 3 first.

## Chapter 2 — The Bells of Mossbrook

The Seedling follows Bristle to a village where the linden bells are ringing by themselves and everyone is standing on the green staring at them. They all turn to look at the stranger. Granny Wren steps out, looks at the key, at Bristle, and says: "Well. Took your time." She calls the dog **Bristle**, as if she has always known him. Bread first, questions after: the Seedling is fed in her crooked cottage, meets Pip the crow and a sulking hearthwisp (Granny and her neighbour quarrelled about the bells). Tobin the miller's son shows up to gawp at "the Tree's child". Granny Wren explains, a little: Seedlings, the Hush, the Tree that takes in the lost.

**Puzzle:** the hearthwisp will not come out and the bread will not rise. Kind answer: make the two old women make up. Clever answer: find what the hearthwisp actually wants (an apology *and* honey). Brave answer: reach behind the stove (it's warm, not hot; the hearthwisp is surprised into giggling).

**Lore introduced:** Mossbrook customs, hearth bowls, the Seedlings, the Hush in one sentence, the Naming Bowl.
**Ends on:** Granny Wren offers to hold the Naming Bowl tonight, or wait until the Seedling remembers something. And a stranger was seen on the Sun Road asking about the bells.

## Chapter 3 — The Listening Root

Granny Wren takes the Seedling (and Tobin, who insists, and Bristle, who does not need to be asked) to Vael. They climb the Listening Root. With an ear to the bark, the Seedling hears the Tree: wind, water, and for a moment, someone laughing in a voice from another time (a hint of other lives, Leaves and Rings). At the cut limb, the Rings: touching one shows a flash of a green, river-filled land (the Greenreach that was). Granny tells the story of Brother Sun and Sister Moon. High in the crown, the Seedling notices a pale gold **bud** that nobody else sees.

**The Naming.** At the Singing Stream, the Seedling says a name over the bowl. If the family chose a name in chapter 1, the water stays still. If not, Grandmother Owl drops a feather into the bowl, and the name is in a riddle.

**Lore introduced:** the World Tree, the creation myth, the Wardens (in a song), the Ring-Book, the bud (Bloom clock ticks to 2 only if the family notices and tells someone).
**Ends on:** Tell Granny Wren about the bud, keep it a secret for now, or climb higher to look?

## Chapter 4 — The Village in the Roots

Following the Carved Stones (or Bristle, who has been tugging that way for days), the Seedling finds Aun: a village grown from living roots, utterly empty, as if everyone just left. The Root Gate opens when they say thank you. Inside: the Wall of Hands, and in the Grown Hall, **the Sun and Moon Chest**. The key hums like a struck bell. Rootkin glimmers are almost solid here; one, a small one with a crooked moss topknot, follows them. Nettle is with them (she followed the bees) and can see it too.

**Puzzle:** the chest has a sun lock and a moon lock. The key fits the sun side. The moon side needs something else. Clever: the carvings show the moon "opening" when the sun lock is turned *backwards* (toward night). Kind: ask the Rootkin glimmer for help; it presses its tiny hand on the moon. Patient: wait for moonlight through the hole in the roof.

**Inside the chest:** a Rootkin seed the size of an egg, warm; a rolled bark-scroll in a language the Seedling can, impossibly, half read: *Take with one hand. Give with the other.*; and the impression of a second key, shaped like a moon, missing.

**Lore introduced:** the Rootkin, the Giving Law, the Twin Keys, the Great Forgetting hinted ("Why does no one remember who lived here?").
**Ends on:** Plant the seed, keep it, or give it to the Rootkin glimmer?

## Chapter 5 — Market Day

New moon: traders come up the Sun Road. Mossbrook's green fills with stalls. The Seedling meets **Ammaro the Rememberer** with his sand-harp, who sings the Song of the Green That Was: the desert was once a forest, burned by kings who wanted glass. Then **Surveyor Ilse Brand** of Ferrow, polite and neat, walks into the Ironwood Stand with a pot of red paint and starts marking trees. And among the stalls, a smiling peddler selling pretty black flowers. Bristle growls at him.

**Puzzle:** what to do about the red marks? Speak up to the Headwoman (she is tempted by the money). Talk to the surveyor herself (she has never thought about it). Follow the peddler instead (he is Ash Hand). Several right answers; each moves different clocks.

**Lore introduced:** the Ashen Reach and its history, Ferrow and its want of ironwood, the Concord ("no army walks under Vael", but woodcutters aren't an army), first sight of nightbloom.
**Ends on:** the red marks, the peddler, or Ammaro's offer to sing the Seedling's song if they can bring him one true memory?

## Chapter 6 — Lantern Night in the Forest

A Lumai pilgrim, **Sela Dawnreed**, arrives with a mirror carp in a jar, walking to the Tree to ask a question. She shows the Seedling the carp's mirror scales, and in them, for a breath, the Seedling sees a memory: a face, a song, water (a first piece of the true past; pick from `bible.md` section 5 by what the player has shown). That night Mossbrook holds a small Lantern Night for her. Then the carp is stolen. The village blames the stranger. Then the Seedling.

**Puzzle:** a mystery. Clues: wet paw prints too small for Bristle (Pip the crow? no, a thornhog that loves apples, and the jar smelled of apples), a missing apple pie, Tobin's guilty face (he only borrowed the jar to show his little sister). Loyalty: defend Sela. Cleverness: follow the clues. Kindness: go easy on Tobin.

**Lore introduced:** the Mirrorlakes, the Lumai, the Night Without Moon, the Unlanterned (Sela admits she is one).
**Ends on:** Sela asks the Seedling to keep her secret. Do they? And the sick mossback deer at the forest edge has grey eyes.

## Chapter 7 — The Red Marks

Ferrow's woodcutters come with a paper the Headwoman signed. At the same time, a runaway bellows-girl, **Wick**, is found hiding in the Ironwood Stand: she ran from a debt-bond. And the Seedling discovers Old Pell has been cutting ironwood at night to pay his own debt. The grey-eyed mossback deer needs a cure: someone has been planting nightbloom at the forest edge.

This is Book 1's hardest chapter. No one is simply bad. Old Pell is desperate. Surveyor Brand is starting to doubt. Wick is frightened.

**Puzzle:** many threads, the family cannot do everything. Cure the deer (linden honey and stream water; Nettle and the bees help). Hide Wick (loyalty) or find a way to free her debt (cleverness: Founders' Day lottery? Smith Hadda's secret). Stop the woodcutters (bravery: stand in front of the trees; cleverness: the Concord says *no army walks under Vael*, and the paper was signed by the Headwoman, but the Ironwood Stand is under Vael's roots, so it isn't hers to sign; kindness: help Old Pell so he stops cutting).

**Ends on:** whatever they chose not to do still has a cost. The War and Red Marks clocks tick by what they did.

## Chapter 8 — The First Bloom

The night after, Granny Wren takes the Seedling to the Listening Root to meet **Warden-Mother Ysolt**, the blind eldest Keeper. Ysolt tells them, finally, the truth Granny half knew: the bud means the Tree will bloom. The Reckoning is coming. Everything forgotten will come back, and every promise will come due. The Tree woke the Seedling because it needs hands. The Sun Key is theirs; the Moon Key is somewhere in the lakes; and others are looking for both.

From the root, the Seedling sees for a moment the White Stag (the Dawn Stag) standing in the mist, looking at Bristle. Bristle looks back, and wags once.

The Rootkin glimmer from Aun is now solid enough to hold the Seedling's hand.

**The Seedling's first true memory** (from what the family's choices have shown: brave, clever, kind or loyal).
**Ends on:** where will the road go? Three paths for Book 2:
1. **The Lantern Road** — north to the Mirrorlakes with Sela, to find the Moon Key before the Concord renewal on the Lantern Isle.
2. **The Salt Road** — east with Ammaro, to the desert, to learn the Song of the Green and why the wells are failing.
3. **The Iron Road** — to Ferrow with Wick, to free the debt-children and stop the ironwood ships.

Whichever road is not taken still moves: its clocks tick on, and it may be visited by another Leaf, at another time.

## Where it goes after (sketch)

- **Book 2 — The Road** (by choice above). First real journey. First meeting with a Warden. The Concord renewal is approaching.
- **Book 3 — The Concord.** All roads lead to the Lantern Isle. The Concord is signed, or broken. Queen Aurelie. The Ash Hand moves. A second Leaf may start here: a Ward-Child in Solvane.
- **Book 4 — The Unforgetting.** The Hush, found and healed or bound again. The Rootkin begin to return. War.
- **Book 5 — The Reckoning.** The bloom.
- **Other Rings.** A side book set in Aun in the Age of Roots (a Rootkin child), or on the Night Without Moon, whose deeds echo forward.
