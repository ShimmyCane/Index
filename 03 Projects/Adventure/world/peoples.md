# Ondra — Peoples, factions and the web between them

Each people has: who they are, what they value, how they live, what they believe about the world's beginning, festivals, how they talk, their flaw, and people to meet. Then the hidden factions, the forgotten peoples, the web of relations, and the injustices.

Every people has something to admire and something wrong. That is on purpose.

---

## 1. The forest folk of Mossbrook

**Who.** About a hundred people in the village and farms around it. Bee-keepers, millers, woodcarvers, mushroom gatherers, bakers.

**Value.** Belonging, and the forest's good mood. *Borrow and give back.*

**Belief.** The Brother Sun and Sister Moon story (`bible.md`). Hearthwisps in every house. Grandfather Moss in the woods.

**Festivals.** Linden Day (Bloom), the Honey Fair (High Sun), the Candle Night on the Night Without Moon (Long Dark), when a candle is left in every window "for those on the water".

**How they talk.** Proverbs. "A full hive buzzes, an empty hive stings." "Bread first, questions after." "Even the Tree was a seed."

**Flaw.** Suspicious of outsiders, and ashamed of something they never talk about (the refugee boat they turned away at the Breaking). Some are poor enough to sell what is not theirs.

**People to meet**
- **Granny Wren.** The Old Lady. Lives in a crooked cottage at the forest edge with a tame crow. Warm, a little mysterious, speaks in riddles and old sayings, knows more than she says. Secretly the last Keeper of the Root in the village. As a girl she saw the last Seedling wake, and she has waited her whole life for the bells to ring again. *Voice: slow, kind, sly; laughs at her own riddles.*
- **Headwoman Marla Ash.** Runs the village. Honest, tired, practical. Wants Mossbrook kept out of trouble. Will be tempted by Ferrow's money for the village's sake.
- **Tobin Fletch.** The miller's son, about ten. Brags, exaggerates, is afraid of the dark, and is the most loyal friend you could have. The Seedling's first friend.
- **Nettle.** A quiet girl, about nine, who talks to the bees and says they talk back. They do. Sees the Rootkin glimmers too, sometimes.
- **Old Pell.** The woodcutter. Gruff, kind to animals, rude to people. Secretly owes money to Ferrow and has started cutting ironwood at night to sell to them. A traitor because he is desperate, not because he is bad.
- **Smith Hadda.** Big, cheerful, came from Ferrow years ago and never talks about why. A runaway debt-child, grown up.
- **The tame crow, Pip.** Granny Wren's. Steals shiny things, including, once, the key.

## 2. The Keepers of the Root

**Who.** An old order sworn to protect Vael and the forest's neutrality, and to keep the **Ring-Book**, the Tree's year-by-year history. Once hundreds, in grey cloaks lined with green. Now perhaps seven, scattered and old.

**Value.** Patience. Memory. *The Tree remembers; so must we.*

**How they live.** Hermitages in hollow trees. Each Keeper looks after one part of the forest. They meet once a year at the Listening Root.

**Flaw.** They kept the old secrets so long they forgot why. They do not trust anyone young, which is why there are none left.

**People.** Granny Wren. **Brother Quillon**, a Keeper who has not been seen for a year (his empty cloak is a Book 1 hook). **Warden-Mother Ysolt**, the eldest, blind, who lives at the Listening Root.

## 3. The Tallenai — Caravan Houses of the Ashen Reach

**Who.** Desert people in great families called Houses, crossing the sand in caravans of longwalkers (camels) carrying salt, dates, glass, books and stories.

**Value.** Hospitality and memory. A guest is sacred for three days. A promise over water cannot be broken.

**How they live.** Felt tents on the move, mud-brick cities at the oases. Sand-scarves coloured by House. Tea in three small glasses: *the first strong as life, the second sweet as friendship, the third soft as sleep.*

**Belief.** The world is **the Given**. The Greenreach was the River Serpent's garden, burned by greedy kings; one day it will be given back. Every Rememberer sings **the Song of the Green That Was**, and the desert does not entirely forget, because songs are harder to erase than books.

**Festivals.** The Night of First Water (when the year's first rain falls, everyone dances in it). The Long Telling (seven nights of stories at Ibbasar).

**How they talk.** Formal, generous, with blessings: "May your well be deep." "I have drunk at your fire" means "I owe you."

**Houses.**
- **House of the Seven Wells.** Old, proud, poorer now: they built the qanats and are losing them.
- **House Amberline.** Traders and scholars; run the Hall of Ten Thousand Pages.
- **House Sahrune.** The Well-Lords, newly rich: they bought the failing wells' water rights and sell water by the cup.
- **The Green House (forgotten).** A seventh house that refused the glass trade and was erased in the Great Forgetting. Their sign was a leaf.

**Flaw.** Feuds that last generations. House Sahrune's water sales are legal by Tallenai law, and that is the problem.

**People.**
- **Ammaro the Rememberer.** Old, thin, laughing, travels with a sand-harp. Comes to Mossbrook on Market Day. Knows hundreds of years of songs, including one about a child asleep in the roots of the Tree. *Voice: musical, rhythmic, sings parts.*
- **Well-Lord Vesh Sahrune.** Polite, rich, believes he is saving the desert by making water cost what it is worth.
- **Lessa of the Seven Wells.** A girl of twelve who dives into failing qanats to find the blockages. Brave and stubborn. (Book 2 friend.)

## 4. The Lumai — Lantern Folk of the Mirrorlakes

**Who.** Lake people living on floating islands, stilt houses and boats. Fishers, gardeners of floating fields, lantern-makers, divers.

**Value.** Stillness and remembrance. They believe the lakes are the Moon's tears, so they owe her their memories.

**How they live.** Leg-rowing boats, floating gardens, reed houses rebuilt every year. Children learn to swim before they walk.

**Belief.** The world is **the Mirror**: the world under the water is the true one, where the Moon's Children live and the drowned are at peace.

**Festivals.** **Lantern Night**, at the full moon after Harvest: everyone floats a paper lantern carrying a memory they want the Moon to keep. Thousands of lights on the water.

**How they talk.** Softly. They pause before answering, to let the water settle.

**The Lantern Synod.** Nine **Stillkeepers** who rule Velomar from the Moonhouse. Kind, careful, frightened. They keep the **Moon Key**, and they know the truth of the Great Forgetting, because their ancestors made the great mirror through which Orrin the Bright bound the Hush. Out of shame, they have kept it secret for four hundred years.

**Flaw.** Anyone who "speaks ill of the Moon" (questions the Synod) is **Unlanterned**: their lantern is put out and they must live on the outer reeds.

**People.**
- **Sela Dawnreed.** A young Lumai pilgrim, about fifteen, walking to Vael with a mirror carp in a glass jar to ask the Tree a question. Arrives in Mossbrook in Book 1. Gentle, and secretly Unlanterned.
- **First Stillkeeper Oma Tallow.** Old, kind, carries the Synod's secret like a stone.
- **Fen**, an Unlanterned smuggler with a laugh like a duck. Knows every channel.

## 5. Solvane — the Sunward Court

**Who.** The heirs of the old empire: a great city and its farms. Courtiers, scholars, priests of the Sun, soldiers of the Sunguard, shopkeepers, divers in the Drowned Quarter.

**Value.** Order, beauty, learning, rank. *Everything in its place under the sun.*

**How they live.** Rank is everything: the **Thousand Steps** of the palace decide where you may stand. The **Examinations** let any child become a scholar-official, in principle. In practice the fee is high.

**Belief.** The Sun is the true ruler of the world and the Throne is his hand. The Moon's grief is called "the Flood"; the Glass Kings are remembered as great, and their fall as a tragedy, not a punishment.

**Festivals.** **Sun's Crowning** at midsummer: a gold procession, the Queen shown to the people. **The Day of Steps**, when rank can change.

**How they talk.** Elaborate, polite, every sentence a little bow. Insults are hidden in compliments.

**The Ward-Children.** Every noble house, and every people Solvane once ruled, must send one child to be raised at court as a "ward". Really, they are hostages for good behaviour. Some have not seen home in years.

**Flaw.** Pride, and a history it refuses to look at.

**People.**
- **Queen Aurelie the Seventh.** Eleven. Clever, lonely, kind, allowed to decide nothing. Writes secret letters to nobody and throws them in the river. A future friend for the children (Book 2 or 3).
- **Lord Regent Cassian Morrow.** Charming, generous, always smiling. Wants to "make Solvane great again", which means bringing back sun-glass. Is using the Ash Hand, and thinks he controls them.
- **High Examiner Tamsin Vey.** Honest scholar. Secretly helps poor children pass the examination.
- **Corin**, a Drowned Quarter diver boy who finds a piece of glass with a Rootkin hand-print in it.

## 6. Ferrow — the Iron Compact

**Who.** The free city of smiths, shipwrights, printers, merchants and bankers.

**Value.** Freedom (their own), work, progress. *No crown over us.*

**How they live.** Guilds for everything. A Free School for guild children, the first printing press in Ondra, the best ships. Below that, the **debt-bond**: a family that borrows and cannot repay gives a child's work to the guild until the debt is paid. Debt-children work the great bellows.

**Belief.** The world is **the Market**: things that have a price get looked after. They think the Tree is just a very big tree, and the Giving Law a nice old saying.

**Festivals.** **Founders' Day**, when the gates are opened and the debts of seven families are forgiven by lottery.

**How they talk.** Quick, blunt, numbers in every sentence. "What's it worth?"

**Flaw.** They were slaves who became masters, and do not see it.

**People.**
- **Guildmaster Orsk Talleran.** Big, hearty, self-made, believes ironwood ships will make everyone rich. Not cruel; just never sees the people at the bottom.
- **Surveyor Ilse Brand.** Neat, polite, carries red paint. Comes to the forest in Book 1 to mark ironwood. Starts to doubt.
- **Wick.** A bellows-girl of about ten who runs away and hides in the forest. (Book 1, chapter 7.)

## 7. The Tallgrass Clans

**Who.** Horse people of the grass sea in nine clans. Herders, eagle-hunters, riders.

**Value.** Freedom of the open sky, honour, the horse. *The sky is the roof of all.*

**How they live.** Felt tents moved with the seasons. Mare's-milk feasts. Wrestling, archery, horse races at the summer gathering. Throat-singing that sounds like wind and river at once.

**Belief.** The Wind Mare ran the first circle round the world, and the grass grew where her hooves touched.

**Flaw.** Pride and quick tempers; the clans quarrel with each other.

**People.** **Khatun Bayra**, chief of the clans, fierce and fair. **Temur**, her youngest son, who wants to see the forest.

## 8. The Yanari, the Wayfinders, the Velde

Further away; sketched for later books.

- **The Yanari** (Stairstep Mountains): terrace farmers, knot-keepers, bridge-weavers. Each village rebuilds its grass bridge every year together, and *ayni*-like reciprocity is their whole law: you help with my harvest, I help with yours. Their cords remember the Rootkin.
- **The Wayfinders** (Scattered Isles): navigators who read stars, swells and birds. Children learn the star-paths as songs. They believe the world is round.
- **The Velde** (White North): reindeer herders who sing a person into a song. They say the northern lights are Vael's crown.

## 9. Hidden factions

- **The Ash Hand.** A secret society: the world is rotten, so let the Reckoning burn it clean. They want both keys, to open the Tree's heart and release the sun-fire. They use **nightbloom**, a black flower from the Glass Flats, whose scent brings the **grey sleep**. Members hide in every people: a peddler, a courtier, a Keeper gone bad. Their leader, **the Ember Mother**, is masked. (Storyteller only: who she is is open. Options: a Lumai Stillkeeper who wants the Synod's shame burned away; Lessa's lost aunt from the Seven Wells; Brother Quillon's sister. Decide in Book 2 or later.)
- **The Glass Remnant.** Descendants of the Glass Kings' sorcerer-court, hiding in Solvane's Drowned Quarter. They want sun-glass and the throne back, and think Orrin the Bright is not dead, only asleep in glass.
- **The Unlanterned.** Lake outcasts. Smugglers and truth-tellers. Will help anyone the Synod would not.

## 10. Forgotten peoples

- **The Rootkin.** Small (teapot to knee height), bark-skinned, moss-haired, eyes like drops of honey. Spoke in hums and creaks. Grew houses, bridges and whole villages from living roots. Faded in the Great Forgetting: not dead, *unremembered*. A few linger as glimmers that only children, Seedlings and the very old can see, and only for a blink. Every time someone truly remembers one, it grows a little more solid. Their sign is the seed-eye. **Book 1's quiet heart is the Seedling starting to remember them.**
- **The Moon's Children (Ilenai).** Silver-haired night folk who lived on the moonlit hills of the old valley. On the Night Without Moon they walked into the rising water singing. The Lumai believe they live in the Underlake.
- **The Green House.** The seventh desert House, erased for refusing the glass trade.
- **The Riverfolk of the Greenreach.** Fishers and boat-builders of the green rivers. When the rivers died, they walked into the desert and became, partly, the Tallenai.

## 12. The injustices running through the world

Each one is a thread the children can find, understand, and push against. None is solved quickly.

| **Water sold by the cup** | Desert herders | House Sahrune | Ammaro's song at Market Day |
