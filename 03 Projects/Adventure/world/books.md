# Ondra — The ten books

A rough plan for about ten books, around 90 chapters. **This is Athena's story.** It will not be followed exactly: every choice can split the story, so think of this as the trunk of a tree with many branches.

---

## 1. How the story branches

Stories fragment over time. Ondra plans for that.

```
Book 1 ── Book 2 ─┬─ 2·Lantern ─┐
 The Waking       ├─ 2·Salt ────┼── Book 3 ─┬─ 3·Kept ───┐
                  └─ 2·Iron ────┘  Concord  └─ 3·Broken ─┴── Book 4 ── Book 5 ── … ── Book 10
                                                          (every branch still reaches the trunk events)
```

- **The trunk** is what happens in every version: the bud, the Concord renewal on the Lantern Isle, the war people make, the bloom (when Jimmy switches it on), the Glass King, the ending's big question.
- **A branch** is a version that a big choice opens. It is named after the book and the choice: `2·Lantern`, `2·Salt`, `2·Iron`, then `3·Kept` or `3·Broken`, and so on. Branches can split again (`2·Salt·Glasswight`).
- **The family plays one branch at a time.** The branches they don't take are not wasted: they stay "in the Tree". They can be played later as one of Athena's other lives, as a replay, or by another family member, and what happens in them can echo.
- **Branches meet again at trunk events,** but carry their differences: who Athena's friends are, which promises she kept, which clocks are high, which people trust her.
- **Small choices don't branch.** They change details, standing and clocks inside the same branch. Only a choice that changes where the story *goes* opens a new branch.
- The storyteller records the current branch in the `setting` entry **Branch** (for example `2·Salt`) and starts each new book with a `plan` entry for that branch. A later change can add a `branch` column to chapters so several versions can live side by side (see `todo.md`).

## 2. The Reckoning: switch, timeline and teasers

- **A manual switch.** The `setting` entry **Reckoning** is `off` until Jimmy turns it on under the hood. The storyteller never starts it alone.
- **Ten chapters to arrive.** When Jimmy writes `on (from chapter N)`, the storyteller builds toward the bloom for ten chapters, and the Tree blooms in chapter N + 10.
- **Suggested timing:** switch on around chapter 44 (late Book 5), so the bloom is the heart of Book 6. If it is switched on earlier or later, books stretch or squeeze around it; Books 4 and 5 can take side branches while the switch is off.
- **While off,** the story stays light (`bible.md` section 8), and the Bloom clock stops at 5 / 6.

At least six. Glimpses, never explanations.

## 3. The books

Chapter numbers are rough. Each book has a real ending and a question that opens the next.

### Book 1 — The Waking (chapters 1–8)
Athena wakes in the roots with no name. Mossbrook, Granny Wren, the Listening Root, the Naming Bowl, Aun and the Sun and Moon Chest, Market Day, a stolen carp, the red marks, the first bloom-talk. Details in `book1.md`. **Ends on:** three roads.

### Book 2 — The Road (9–17), three versions
- **2·Lantern** — north to the Mirrorlakes with Sela. Leg-rowers, floating gardens, Lantern Night on the lakes, the Unlanterned on the outer reeds, the Synod's shame and a rumour of the Moon Key. Levity: Fen the smuggler's duck laugh, reed-sprites tangling everyone's hair.
- **2·Salt** — east with Ammaro into the Ashen Reach. Guest-right, singing dunes, Lessa diving in the failing wells, a glasswight's bargain, the Song of the Green That Was, Well-Lord Vesh selling water. Levity: a longwalker that will only move if called beautiful.
- **2·Iron** — to Ferrow with Wick. Canals, bellows halls, the Free School, Founders' Day lottery, Guildmaster Talleran, the ironwood ship. Levity: Pip loose in the Counting House.
**Trunk:** an invitation (or a summons) to the Concord renewal on the Lantern Isle. **Ends on:** whether Athena goes, and whom she brings.

### Book 3 — The Concord (18–26), two versions
All roads lead to the Lantern Isle. Five powers, one cutting of Vael, and a child queen who has never had a friend. Athena meets Queen Aurelie. Regent Cassian smiles. The Ash Hand moves: a poisoned cup (the grey sleep), a stolen thing. The Concord is renewed (**3·Kept**) or breaks (**3·Broken**), depending on what Athena and her friends did. **Ends on:** a friend in the grey sleep, and a cure that only grows far away.

*Other life, optional:* **Aun in the Age of Roots** (see section 4).

### Book 4 — The Unforgetting (27–35)
Into the Stairstep Mountains to find the Hush. The Yanari knot-keepers read Athena's past by touch. The missing seventh verse of the Wardens' song. The Hush itself, wounded and sad, and the choice: heal it, bind it, or befriend it. The Rootkin begin to return, a few at a time. Meanwhile the War clock climbs. **Ends on:** a first Rootkin walking solidly into Mossbrook, and soldiers on the Sun Road.

### Book 5 — The Drums (36–44)
The War of the Broken Concord begins (if the Concord was kept, the Ash Hand breaks it with a lie). Not battles: closed roads, dear bread, a spy in the village, refugees, Tobin's cousin gone to the army. Mossbrook faces its old shame and must decide whether to open its doors. Levity is essential here: a thornhog asleep in a general's tent, the whisperfox mocking a herald. **Suggested moment to switch the Reckoning on.** **Ends on:** the war reaching the forest's edge, and the bud splitting open.

*Other life, optional:* **The Night Without Moon** (section 4).

### Book 6 — The Bloom (about ten chapters after the switch)
The Reckoning. The Tree blooms; the Hush breaks; everything forgotten comes back at once. Every promise made under the Tree comes due: the guildmaster and the regent are brought to their knees, and a woodcutter, a bee-girl and a bellows-girl stand up. The Sun and Moon share the sky. The Heart Door, the two keys, the sun-fire. Whether the bloom heals or burns depends on everything before. **Ends on:** the world upside down.

### Book 7 — The World Upside Down
After the Reckoning: the Rootkin home, the desert greening (or burning), the old empire crumbling, peasants on new councils, kings learning to plant trees. Athena's true past comes clear. Making peace is harder than winning: old enemies have to share bread. **Ends on:** a crack in a block of black glass under the lake.

### Book 8 — The Glass King
Orrin the Bright wakes from sun-glass in Solvane's Drowned Quarter, with charm and a plan to make the world bright and obedient. The Glass Remnant rises. The darkest book, as the children are older by now; still with light in it. An echo from Athena's other life in the Age of Crowns matters here. **Ends on:** Orrin defeated, or changed, and a Wayfinder's boat on the shore.

### Book 9 — The Far Shore
Across the sea with the Wayfinders, steering by stars and swells, to the land where another tree grows. Another Seedling waits there, and the truth about the Tree's long plan. A life from after the Reckoning folds back on itself. **Ends on:** a choice the Tree itself asks Athena to make.

### Book 10 — The Giving
The end of the first great story. The Giving Law spoken again, by everyone. Athena chooses where she belongs: to stay, to return to the roots, or to become a Keeper. Epilogue across the Rings: a glimpse of every life she lived. **Ends on:** a new bud, and a new Seedling stirring in the moss, for whoever plays next.

## 4. Athena's other lives

Options for Athena to explore, unlocked as the story goes. Each is a short book (three to six chapters) set in another Ring. What she does there echoes into the present.

| Life | Ring | Unlocks after | Who she is | What echoes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Moss-in-Hand** | Age of Roots | Book 3 | A human girl in Aun, learning to grow houses with Rootkin friends | A hand-print on the Wall of Hands that fits hers; a grove she plants; Rootkin who remember her name |
| **The Glassmaker's Apprentice** | Age of Crowns | Book 4 | An apprentice in a sun-glass workshop as the Greenreach burns | A flaw she hides in the sun-glass, which matters in Book 8; a desert family who owes her |
| **The Night Without Moon** | Ring 2,700 | Book 5 | A girl in drowning Solvane, and the refugee boat that Mossbrook turned away | Whether the Great Heron carried someone to the Tree; Mossbrook's candle in the window |
| **After** | After the Reckoning | Book 8 | Athena grown up, sent back by the Tree | Why she woke with no memory (one of the possible truths in `bible.md` section 5) |

Other family members can play other lives too: a Rootkin child, a Ward-Child in Solvane, a Tallgrass rider.

## 5. Notes for the storyteller

- Track the branch, the clocks, the Reckoning switch and Athena's standing before each chapter.
- Each book gets one Warden, one new people, one injustice, one funny companion, and one big promise.
- Keep the levity: one light moment per chapter, more in the dark books.
- Untaken branches are seeds. Mention one, now and then, as a rumour of what might have been.
