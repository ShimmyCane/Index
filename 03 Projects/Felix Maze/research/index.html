💭 What I was thinking (round 1)

Felix's own mazes are free and flowing. The danger with printed mazes is that they all look like the same square grid, and he learns that that is what a maze is. So the plan starts from variety: 100 kinds of maze from art, nature, systems, music and famous places, each tagged by its shape, its line work and its tricks.

Three ideas shaped the plan:

  1. The algorithm is the brush. Try the buttons on the left: the same grid feels completely different depending on how it was grown. The Wanderer looks most like a person drew it, so it's the starting point for Felix. The Mix setting is one knob that turns easy into hard.
  2. Not everything should be a grid. Researchers have grown mazes like meandering rivers (Pedersen and Singh) and made them swirl along a picture (Xu and Kaplan). That's the road to a real Starry Night maze, and closest to how Felix draws.
  3. A good maze is a picture and a puzzle at once. Wide paths, a real goal (mouse → cheese), a few tempting wrong turns at both ends, and room to color it in afterwards.

Black-and-white printing turns colors into patterns: dots, stripes, crosshatch.

Still open: the questions in convo v0.1. The full notes are in maze_research.md, the list in inspirations.md, the knobs in variations.md.

📖 Maze words
WordIn plain words
LabyrinthOne path, no choices. You can't get lost; you just walk it.
MazeThe path splits. You choose, and you can go the wrong way.
Perfect mazeExactly one way between any two spots. No loops.
Braid mazeHas loops. You get lost going around in circles, not by hitting walls. (Slide “Loops” up.)
Dead endA branch that stops at a wall.
DecoyA wrong branch that looks right for a long time.
RiverHow long the halls run before they split. Wanderer: high river. Spreader: low river.
WeavePaths that go over and under each other, on bridges.

Growing curvesA line that wiggles, grows and pushes away from itself, like a river or brain coral. (Pedersen & Singh, 2006)

🧀 What makes a maze fun
  1. A picture and a puzzle. If the picture wins it isn't a maze; if the puzzle wins it's dull.
  2. Tempting branches at both ends, so starting from the cheese isn't a shortcut.
  3. Fair: makes him think, never makes him quit.
  4. A real goal: mouse to cheese beats arrow to dot.
  5. One world per sheet: walls, start, goal and decorations all belong together.
  6. Something to find: a bridge, a creature, a hidden picture, a fact at the bottom.

For a young solver

  • Wide paths first, about ½ inch. Narrow paths tire small hands.
  • Only 1–2 dead ends to start. Make it harder by making it bigger.
  • A sheet protector and a dry-erase marker make mistakes free.

On 11×17 the drawing area is about 10 × 14 inches. A ½-inch path is roughly a 13 × 18 grid; a ⅜-inch path roughly 20 × 28. These numbers come from one teacher site, so we'll test them with a real sheet.

🌿 Why getting lost feels good

Jimmy's note: every kid has a soft spot for mazes. It's fun to get lost. Like a coloring book for grown-ups, a maze quiets the mind.

That idea is old. Walking labyrinths, like the one on the floor of Chartres Cathedral, have long been used for slow, quiet walking, a kind of moving meditation. A labyrinth has no wrong turns, so the mind can rest. A maze asks you to choose, so the mind wakes up. A good series can give Felix both: some sheets to wander, some to solve.
